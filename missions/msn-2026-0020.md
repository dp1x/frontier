# MSN-2026-0020 — notes

Mission: independent adversarial review of the corrected pycose ordering scope,
plus a local probe closing the three named measurement gaps.

Closed 2026-10-02 as `verified`.

## What the mission actually produced

The session set out to strengthen `fnd-2026-0014` before any upstream report.
It did the opposite. The probe strengthened the *measurements*; the review
destroyed the *framing*.

## Thread B — the probe (`exp-2026-0034`, `obs-2026-0046`)

Closed all three queued gaps, and found a fourth nobody had listed:

1. **Clean mixed int+tstr.** Used tstr labels pycose does not register
   (`zzz`, `mmm`, `qqq`). The forward/reversed pair over one logical map is the
   real control: sorting would make both emissions identical.
2. **Bare API vs adapter.** Byte-identical everywhere. The adapter is exonerated;
   `obs-2026-0045`'s "adapter-mediated only" caveat no longer limits anything.
3. **`phdr_encoded`.** Accepts pre-encoded bytes, returns them verbatim. This was
   the open Qwen Turn-3 item, and it turned out to be answerable in one grep of
   `cosebase.py` rather than an external research round trip.
4. **Found gap: do the vectors distinguish §4.2.1 from §4.2.3?** No — every
   vector in the corpus so far has equal-length key encodings, where the two
   rules agree. The `{1000, "z"}` vector discriminates and proves pycose applies
   **neither** rule. The critic independently confirmed no int-only
   discriminator exists at all.

Also settled: `cbor2`'s `canonical=True` is §4.2.3, not §4.2.1. So the obvious
one-line fix does not work — worth knowing before anyone proposes it upstream.

## Thread A — the adversarial review (`rev-2026-0017`)

Spawned as a non-synthesizer `adversarial-critic` whose explicit goal was to
disprove the corrected scope. Verdict: **REFUTES**.

It reproduced every measurement, then attacked the framing and landed four
fatal hits:

- **The RFC 9052 §9 quote in the finding is not in RFC 9052.** The
  map-key-sorting clause is RFC 8949 §4.2.1 text spliced into a §9 quotation.
  The orchestrator re-fetched RFC 9052 and confirmed this independently rather
  than trusting the subagent.
- **§9 does not require map ordering.** It narrows the alignment to three
  restrictions — scope (Sig/Enc/MAC structure), definite minimum lengths, no
  duplicate keys. Ordering is not among them. §4.4 carries `body_protected` as
  an opaque `empty_or_serialized_map`. And §3 says the bstr wrapping exists to
  avoid "all parties needing to be able to do a common canonical encoding".
- **It is already known upstream.** PR #91, "Avoid re-encoding of protected
  header", merged 2022-11-08 by the maintainer, following issue #82: *"COSE
  does not enforce any deterministic encoding for the protected header."*
  `phdr_encoded` **is** that shipped fix. `novelty unchecked` → known and
  deliberate.
- **"Mutually unverifiable" is false.** With real P-256 crypto, a
  conformantly-signed message **verifies** in pycose, because decode preserves
  received bytes. The breakage is one-directional: a pycose *signer* emits bytes
  a conformant *verifier* rejects.

## What changed in the artifacts

`fnd-2026-0014` stays `verified_conclusion`, but for a materially different
claim: a measured, reproducible, non-canonical protected-bucket encoding whose
status against the RFC is an **open interpretation**. Its `SPEC_VIOLATION`
classification, its symmetric impact claim, and its pending maintainer-report
step are all withdrawn and recorded as withdrawn.

## The lesson worth keeping

`fnd-2026-0014` had been corrected twice before — once for scope, once for a
stale promotion claim — and both corrections made it *stronger*. This
correction made it weaker, and it was the first review anyone ran against its
current scope.

A misquotation survived two correction passes and a promotion. It survived
because every session checked whether the *measurement* was still accurate and
nobody re-fetched the RFC text. "Fix the scope" and "fix the framing" are
different jobs, and the second one had no owner until the scope was finally
stable enough for an adversary to attack.

The negative result is preserved deliberately. `AGENTS.md` treats a disproved
hypothesis as durable evidence, and this one just removed the repo's strongest
candidate for external impact.

## Open, deliberately not closed

The reading that §9's narrowing sentence is **normative** rather than editorial
is interpretation, not citation. It is the single question that would revive a
residual spec-violation reading, and it needs a human who reads specifications
for a living — or an errata discussion — not another agent pass.
